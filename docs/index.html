Section 00

Executive Abstract

A five-sentence structured summary of the question, data contract, validation methodology, headline results, and operational utility.

Can historical search performance, content freshness, engagement metrics, and visibility signals prioritize content pages that need optimization before performance decays? Using an anonymized portfolio of 30,000 published pages across 32 enterprise clients from the FlyRank Search Intelligence dataset, we engineered a leakage-safe feature space spanning search demand, user engagement, and content age. We evaluated heuristic baseline rules alongside supervised classification and continuous regression models under strict client-holdout cross-validation to prevent inter-client leakage. In out-of-sample client-holdout validation, our Random Forest classifier achieved a Precision@50 of 0.740 (a 3.08× lift over the business rule baseline of 0.240) and an ROC AUC of 0.750, while continuous trend regression revealed that historical signals alone do not reliably predict continuous percentage changes (R² ≈ -0.01 to -0.24). We synthesized these findings into a transparent decision-support action playbook that assigns tiered interventions (refresh immediately, review CTR, review engagement, monitor) with verifiable reason codes, giving editorial teams an evidence-backed queue for resource allocation.

Section 01

Introduction & Problem Framing

The operational dilemma: allocating scarce editorial resources to high-impact content optimization.

The Operational Decision

Enterprise publishers, SaaS knowledge bases, and content networks manage catalogs containing tens of thousands of published articles. Editorial and technical SEO resources are strictly constrained: an editorial team can realistically audit, rewrite, and refresh only 20 to 50 URLs per week.

Historically, teams prioritize articles using crude heuristics: sorting by total traffic, looking at publish date, or reacting after page rankings have already plummeted. This capstone creates a repeatable decision-support system: Which published URLs should an editor inspect and refresh this week to recover decaying visibility and capture unharvested clicks?

The Cost of Wrong Calls

Prioritization errors incur steep operational costs in two directions:

  • False Positives (Wasted Editorial Capacity): Rewriting evergreen pages that were temporarily experiencing seasonal fluctuation. Editorial hours are consumed without moving the traffic needle.
  • False Negatives (Silent Traffic Erosion): High-value URLs with strong search demand that quietly slip from position 3 to position 12. Once a page exits top rankings, rebuilding topical authority takes months.

Machine learning outperforms fixed thresholds by isolating non-linear interactions across impression velocity, ranking drift, and engagement decay.

Section 03

Methodology & Validation Design

Grouped cross-validation design, baseline heuristic formulation, and dual modeling paradigms.

Grouped Client-Holdout Split

Standard random train/test splits are deeply flawed for multi-client SEO data. Content URLs from the same client share domain-level backlink authority, hosting speed, CMS structure, and site-wide Google trust. Randomly splitting URLs across train and test partitions results in site-level information leakage, artificially inflating test metrics.

We partitioned the data using a strict Client Holdout Split, reserving 20% of clients (6 complete client portfolios, 5,892 URLs) exclusively for out-of-sample evaluation. No client portfolio appeared in both training and test sets.

The Heuristic Baseline Rule

To ensure honest evaluation, we compared all machine learning models against a transparent business heuristic baseline mirroring standard enterprise SEO scoring:

Score = 0.40 × rank(search_volume) +
        0.30 × (1 - rank(avg_position)) +
        0.30 × (1 - rank(days_since_update))

This baseline flags high-demand, high-ranking pages that are aging. Its out-of-sample Precision@50 is 0.240, confirming that aging pages often remain stable evergreen assets.

Section 05

Limitations & Honest Framing

Explicit boundaries on what this research can and cannot claim.

The Claim Ladder: Decision-Support, Not Causal Proof

Observed Associations, Not Causal Guarantees: This study analyzed observational, cross-sectional search data over a trailing 90-day window. We cannot claim that executing an editorial refresh will cause rankings or traffic to recover. The model provides an evidence-based queue of URLs that exhibit signals of decay and under-monetized demand, functioning strictly as a human-in-the-loop decision-support tool.

No Claim to Google's Internal Algorithm: Machine learning models trained on search console aggregates identify statistical regularities in historical performance. We make zero claim of "reverse engineering" Google's proprietary search ranking algorithms or predicting unannounced search core updates.

Survivorship and Active Item Filtering: The dataset captures published content that registered search visibility. Completely orphaned pages with zero historical impressions are outside the observable domain.

External Volatility

SERP layout shifts (AI Overviews, video carousels, local packs) alter click-through rates independent of content quality or on-page relevance.

Catalog Disparities

Client domains vary in technical architecture and crawl budget. Grouped validation reduces inter-client leakage, but performance varies across niche industries.

Qualitative Signals

User intent alignment, brand trust, and authoritative accuracy require human editorial judgment that cannot be fully captured by quantitative tabular signals.

Section 06

Ranked Content Action Playbook

The operational engine: turning probabilistic model outputs into concrete, prioritized editorial workflows.

Priority Opportunity Scoring Formula
Opportunity Score = 0.50 × (Pdecline × 100) + 0.30 × percentile(log(impressions)) + 0.20 × GapCTR
Combines calibrated model decline risk (50%), total historical search demand volume (30%), and the gap between expected and actual click-through rate (20%) into a normalized 0–100 index.
Figure 05
Portfolio Action Tier Distribution
Operational Focus: 43.6% of pages are marked monitor (do not waste resources). Only 27.3% require immediate content refresh, while 22.2% require metadata CTR review.
Editorial Playbook Matrix

Intervention Taxonomy

Every flagged URL is routed to a specialized playbook based on its underlying failure mode:

  • Refresh & Review CTR (22.2%): Rewrite title tags and meta descriptions to match evolving search intent; test schema markup.
  • Refresh Immediately (27.3%): Substantive content overhaul: update outdated figures, rewrite stale sections, add expert commentary.
  • Review Engagement (6.6%): Fix poor scroll depth (<30%): break up text walls, embed diagrams, improve mobile page speed.
  • Monitor (43.6%): Stable or growing pages. Prevent unnecessary edits from disrupting existing algorithmic rankings.
